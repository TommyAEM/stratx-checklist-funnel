The 7-Gate Strategy Validation Checklist

A trading strategy is not "good" because its equity curve looks good. It is good if — and only if — it survives every gate below. Run any strategy through these gates in order. The first gate it fails is where the work is.

GATE 1Data integrity

Every downstream number inherits the quality of the data underneath it. A backtest on broken data is fiction with decimals.

  • Prices come from the venue you'll actually trade, at the timeframe you'll actually trade
  • Volume is real — indicators like VWAP silently break on zero-volume feeds
  • No gaps, splices or repaired history hiding inside the series
  • Spreads, commissions and slippage are modelled, not ignored

GATE 2Sample size

Ten winning trades prove nothing. Small samples make random luck look like edge.

  • Enough trades that one streak can't dominate the statistics (dozens at minimum, more is better)
  • Trades spread across different market regimes — trending, ranging, volatile, quiet
  • No single month or single instrument carrying the whole result

GATE 3Positive expectancy after costs

An edge that disappears when you subtract the spread was never an edge.

  • Expectancy per trade is positive after spread, commission and slippage
  • Reward-to-risk and win rate make sense together — neither alone tells you anything
  • The result survives a modest worsening of assumed costs

GATE 4Drawdown discipline

The question is not "how much does it make?" but "what will it feel like to hold through its worst run — and will the account survive it?"

  • Maximum historical drawdown is known, and small enough to actually sit through
  • Longest losing streak is known — and you've asked whether you'd keep following the system at that point
  • Position sizing is derived from drawdown, not from optimism

GATE 5Parameter stability

If performance collapses when a setting moves slightly, you didn't find an edge — you found a coincidence and tuned into it.

  • Neighbouring parameter values perform similarly — a plateau, not a lone spike
  • The strategy's logic has a reason to work; it isn't just the best of 10,000 random combinations
  • Fewer parameters beat more parameters — every knob is an opportunity to overfit

GATE 6Monte Carlo robustness

Your backtest is one sequence of history. Monte Carlo asks: what about the thousands of sequences that could have happened instead?

  • Trade order reshuffled thousands of times — the strategy stays profitable across the distribution
  • Worst-case simulated drawdown is still survivable
  • Results don't hinge on a handful of outlier trades

GATE 7Independent reproduction

The strongest test: does the result hold on data the strategy has never seen, reproduced by a process that has no reason to flatter it?

  • Out-of-sample period held back and tested only once
  • Walk-forward: re-validated as new data arrives, not validated once and trusted forever
  • Backtest and live/demo execution agree — divergence between the two is a defect, not a detail
The rule that makes the checklist work: a strategy that fails any gate is not "almost ready" — it is not ready. A documented weakness is still a weakness. Fix it, or bin the strategy. There are always more strategies; there is only one account.
